Hi! I'm Roxane (Rox) Dumont, a Postdoctoral Researcher at the Unit of Population Epidemiology (UEP) at the Geneva University Hospitals (HUG).

I am an epidemiologist interested in how the places we live, the work we do and the habits we build shape our health over time. My work sits at the intersection of population cohorts, spatial analysis and behavioral health: I want to understand not only who is at risk, but where, when and why, so that prevention can reach the people who need it most. I believe that robust, transparent and reproducible data analysis is one of the most powerful tools we have to reduce health inequalities.

### Main fields of interest

Population Epidemiology · Spatial Epidemiology · Sedentary Behavior & Physical Activity · Social Determinants of Health · Workplace Discrimination · Nutrition & Ultra-processed Foods · Addictive & Digital Behaviors · Adolescent Health

### Main skills

Longitudinal Cohort Analysis · Survival Analysis · Spatial & Spatiotemporal Statistics · Mixed-effects Models · Mediation Analysis · Network Analysis · R · Data Visualization · Scientific Writing · Public Speaking

### Work and research

My research draws on two large population-based studies in Geneva: **Bus Santé**, a long-running cohort of Geneva adults, and **Specchio-COVID19**, a digital population cohort launched during the pandemic. Using these data, I study:

- **Sedentary behavior and health:** how sitting time is distributed across Geneva in space and time, how it relates to green and blue environments, and how it is associated with all-cause mortality.
- **Work and mental health:** how workplace discrimination relates to anxiety and wellbeing, and through which pathways.
- **Diet:** patterns of highly processed food consumption and their associations with health.
- **Addictive and digital behaviors:** alcohol, substance use and screen-based behaviors, and their links with personality traits and anxiety.
- **Women's health:** oral contraceptive use, side effects and discontinuation.

### Background

I completed my PhD on the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on the health of children and adolescents, using the SEROCoV-KIDS cohort. This work received the PSLS Annual Thesis Prize 2025.

Since my PhD, I have been a postdoctoral researcher at the UEP, working with Prof. Idris Guessous and Dr. Mayssam Nehme. My work has been published in journals including Nature Communications, BMJ Open, PLOS ONE and Swiss Medical Weekly.

### Work philosophy

I am curious by nature and enjoy bringing together epidemiology, statistics, geography and the social sciences to answer questions that matter for public health. I care about research that is useful beyond the paper: findings that can inform prevention strategies and public policy. I enjoy working in interdisciplinary teams, mentoring students, and sharing results with both scientific and general audiences.
